# Intellectual lineage

This repository is an original synthesis. It does not claim endorsement by the people or organizations listed below and does not reproduce proprietary templates.

Key influences include:

- **Jim Kalbach** — alignment diagrams, experience mapping as an alignment practice, and jobs-to-be-done.
- **Marc Stickdorn and service-design collaborators** — service design, journey management, service blueprinting, and the shift from project artifacts to ongoing management.
- **Chris Risdon and Patrick Quattlebaum** — experience orchestration across channels, touchpoints, ecosystems, journeys, principles, opportunities, and future visions.
- **Sarah Gibbons / Nielsen Norman Group** — practical distinction between journey maps and service blueprints and the frontstage/backstage service model.
- **Kerry Bodine** — current-state/future-state mapping, hypothesis validation, experience ecosystems, and transformation activation.
- **Jacob Morgan** — employee experience across cultural, technological, and physical environments.
- **Ben Whitter** — employee-experience practice as a sustained organizational discipline.
- **Josh Bersin** — cross-functional employee experience and HR/IT/operations implications.
- **Andy Polaine, Lavrans Løvlie, Ben Reason** — service-design practice and service-system thinking.
- **Indi Young** — mental models and understanding behavior independent of organizational structure.
- **Steve Portigal** — interviewing and qualitative research craft.
- **G. Lynn Shostack** — the service blueprint as a design and diagnostic tool (1984).
- **Mary Jo Bitner, Amy Ostrom, Felicia Morgan** — customer-centered service blueprinting with lines of interaction, visibility, and internal interaction.
- **Clayton Christensen and co-authors** — jobs to be done as progress an actor seeks in a circumstance.
- **Anthony Ulwick** — outcome-driven innovation: desired outcomes as measurable success criteria for a job.
- **Alan Klement** — jobs to be done as the struggle for progress and the switch between solutions.
- **Kaoru Ishikawa, fault-tree analysis (NUREG-0492), Sidney Dekker** — branching cause-and-effect analysis and the systems view of error behind the root-cause method.
- **Virginia Braun and Victoria Clarke; Nigel King and colleagues** — thematic and template analysis behind the synthesis method.
- **Kerry Rodden and colleagues; Ron Kohavi, Diane Tang, Ya Xu** — goals–signals–metrics and guardrail metrics behind the metric-tree method.

The repository deliberately combines these traditions with modern operating-model, data, AI, governance, and portfolio-management needs.
