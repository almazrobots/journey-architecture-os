# Research ethics and data handling

Working rules for collecting and handling journey evidence about real people. They set a floor for practice; they are not legal advice. Law varies by jurisdiction and sector: confirm the lawful basis, notices, and retention rules with the organization's privacy or legal function before fieldwork. Where a professional code applies (for example the ICC/ESOMAR International Code for market research), follow it when it sets a higher standard.

The underlying principles are the ones research ethics has long used: respect for persons (informed, voluntary participation; extra protection for those with less autonomy), beneficence (minimize harm, maximize benefit), and justice (fair selection of who bears the burden of research) — as set out in the Belmont Report. Data-protection law such as the GDPR adds purpose limitation, data minimization, storage limitation, and security.

## Before fieldwork

| Check | Rule |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Write the decision the research informs (workflow step 1). Data is collected for that purpose only. A new purpose means a new notice, and new consent where consent is the basis. |
| Lawful basis and notice | Confirmed with privacy/legal; participants get a plain-language notice of who runs the study, why, what is recorded, who sees it, how long it is kept, and how to withdraw. |
| Minimization | List the fields you need for the analysis (segment, role, tenure band). Do not collect names, exact dates of birth, account numbers, or free-text health, financial, or family detail unless the research question requires it. |
| Risk review | Flag sensitive topics (health, finances, legal trouble, immigration status, employment disputes) and vulnerable participants. Where the organization has an ethics or DPIA process, run it. |
| Incentives | Proportionate to time spent, the same for everyone in a segment, and not conditional on what participants say. Incentives must not be large enough to override a reluctant participant's judgment. Customers: never tie incentives to account terms, discounts on disputed bills, or service priority. |

## Consent

- Consent is informed (purpose, recording, use, retention explained before the session), specific (recording and quoting asked separately), and revocable (withdrawal at any time without consequence).
- Record consent with the session reference, not in the transcript.
- Ask again before using material for anything the participant did not agree to: a public talk, a video clip, training material.
- Silence or continuing the session is not consent to recording.

## Vulnerable participants

Take extra care with minors, people in financial or health crisis, people with cognitive impairments, people in dispute with the organization, and anyone whose participation could be observed by someone with power over them.

- Use an adult responsible for a minor's consent in addition to the minor's own agreement, as local rules require.
- Offer breaks and the option to skip questions; stop if distress appears.
- Have a route to real help ready (a case owner, a support line) when the research surfaces an unresolved problem, and keep it separate from the research record.
- Do not recruit people currently in a complaint or legal process about the subject of the study without explicit review.

## Recordings and raw data

- Record only what the analysis needs. Audio is often enough; video adds faces and surroundings.
- Store raw recordings and transcripts in an access-controlled location, not in shared drives, chat, or slide decks.
- Set a retention period at the start (for example, raw recordings deleted after synthesis is accepted; pseudonymized notes kept for the life of the journey model) and record the deletion.
- Access to raw material: the research team only. Stakeholders get findings and pseudonymized quotes.

## Pseudonymization and quoting

- Replace names with session codes (`S03`) at transcription. Keep the key linking codes to people separate, access-restricted, and deleted with the raw data.
- Remove or generalize indirect identifiers in notes and quotes: employer, exact job title in a small team, location, dates, unusual events.
- Pseudonymized data can still identify a person when combined with other data; treat it as personal data.
- Quote only with consent to quoting. Edit for identifiability, not for meaning; mark omissions with an ellipsis.
- Do not pair quotes with attributes that together identify someone ("the only finance admin in the Leeds office").

## AI tools on transcripts

- Use only tools approved by the organization for personal data, with contractual terms that exclude training on your data and state where data is processed and for how long it is retained.
- Pseudonymize before upload where possible. Do not paste raw transcripts into consumer chat tools.
- Tell participants in the notice if recordings will be transcribed or analyzed with automated tools.
- Treat AI-generated summaries and codes as a draft analyst, not a source: every finding still cites the original note and source reference, and a human checks each claim against it.
- Do not use AI tools to infer sensitive attributes (health, emotion, ethnicity) that participants did not state.

## Reporting

- Report counts with their sample ("4 of 6 participants"), never as population percentages.
- Suppress results for groups small enough that a reader could identify individuals.
- When research reveals a harm to a participant (a wrong charge, a safety issue), pass it through the agreed help route with consent; do not hold it back for the study, and do not put identifying detail into the findings.

## Quick checklist

- [ ] Purpose and decision written; data fields limited to it.
- [ ] Lawful basis and notice confirmed with privacy/legal.
- [ ] Consent to participate, record, and quote captured separately.
- [ ] Vulnerable participants and sensitive topics reviewed; help route ready.
- [ ] Incentives proportionate and unconditional.
- [ ] Raw data access-restricted with a retention date.
- [ ] Pseudonymized at transcription; key stored separately.
- [ ] AI tools approved; no raw transcripts in unapproved tools.
- [ ] Small groups suppressed in reporting.
