# Facilitation method

Method for running a journey workshop that produces decisions and labeled hypotheses without turning group opinion into evidence. Use it for the "Before the workshop" section and workflow steps 1–9. The result is a session plan built on `assets/workshop-agenda.md`, plus a decision log, a dissent log, and draft register rows written within two working days.

A workshop is good at three things: pooling what participants know, surfacing where they disagree, and getting a named person to decide. It is poor at establishing what actors experience. Every technique below serves the first three and protects against the fourth.

Grounding: participatory decision-making and decision rules (Kaner 2014); the nominal group technique (Delbecq & Van de Ven 1971); production blocking in group brainstorming (Diehl & Stroebe 1987); anchoring (Tversky & Kahneman 1974); 1-2-4-All (Liberating Structures); consent decision-making (Sociocracy 3.0); the premortem (Klein 2007); psychological safety (Edmondson 1999); decision roles (Rogers & Blenko 2006).

## Step 1 — Pre-work

Complete before invitations go out:

1. **Decision statement.** One sentence naming what must be decided by the end, and by whom: "The Head of Field Operations decides which two onboarding stages get research and redesign next quarter."
2. **Decision rule.** Choose it now (Step 5) and state it in the invitation. Changing the rule in the room reads as manipulation.
3. **Evidence pack.** An extract of the evidence register for the journey: evidence IDs, one-line findings, source type, date, status, and the known unknowns. One page of "what we know, what we believe, what we don't know". Participants read it before the session.
4. **Pre-session silent input.** Ask each participant to submit, individually and before seeing others' input, the three biggest problems they see in the journey and one number they would estimate (for example, weeks until a new technician works alone). Independent estimates collected before discussion are the main defense against anchoring.
5. **Participant map.** Check coverage (actor knowledge, frontstage, backstage, policy, data, measurement, delivery) and map power: who reports to whom, who controls budget, who will carry the decision out. Plan Step 7 from this map.
6. **Roles.** Facilitator (neutral on the content), decision owner, evidence scribe (checks evidence tags live), timekeeper. The facilitator is not the decision owner.

## Step 2 — Evidence handling during the session

Every contribution carries a tag. Agree the tags in the first ten minutes and put them on the board.

| Tag | Means | Requires |
|---|---|---|
| `O` | observed | An evidence ID from the pack, or a named record the participant can produce (report, dashboard, log) |
| `I` | inferred | The observed evidence it rests on and the reasoning, in one line |
| `H` | hypothesis | The participant's role; this is the default for what people know from experience |
| `U` | unknown | The question, written as a question |

Rules:

- No source, no `O`. The evidence scribe checks `O` tags against the pack during breaks and retags without debate.
- A participant's operational knowledge ("in my region, most new technicians shadow for three weeks") is `H`, recorded with the role. If they can produce the record, it becomes candidate evidence and is registered after the session.
- `stakeholder-input` supports `hypothesis` at most, however senior the source.
- `U` is a result, not a failure. Count them at the close; a map with none was not examined.

## Step 3 — Silent generation and anti-anchoring

Group discussion before individual thinking narrows ideas to the first ones spoken and to the most senior voice. Use:

- **Nominal group technique** (Delbecq & Van de Ven): silent individual writing, round-robin sharing one item at a time without discussion, clarification, then independent private voting.
- **1-2-4-All** (Liberating Structures): one minute alone, two in pairs, four in fours, then the whole group hears what fours found. Good for groups of 8–30 when time is short.
- **Brainwriting**: participants write on shared sheets or a board section in parallel; avoids production blocking, the queueing effect that makes spoken brainstorming groups produce fewer ideas than the same people working alone (Diehl & Stroebe).
- **Hidden first pass**: keep the existing map or the pre-session input covered until everyone has written their own view of the stages.
- **Independent estimates first**: for any number, collect private estimates, reveal together, then discuss the spread; never ask "is it about three weeks?".
- **Simultaneous voting**: dots or digital votes revealed at the same moment.
- **Premortem** (Klein) for target designs: "It is a year from now and this redesign failed. Write why." Surfaces objections people would not raise against a proposal.

## Step 4 — Capture dissent

Disagreement is information about where the evidence is thin. Keep a dissent log visible throughout:

| claim | position A (who) | position B (who) | evidence each side cites | what would resolve it | owner | due |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Rules:

- When positions differ and neither side has `O` evidence, the item becomes a research question, not a vote.
- When one side has `O` evidence and the other has experience, the map follows the evidence and the experience is logged as a possible variant (a segment, region, or time period the evidence may not cover).
- Ask explicitly before converging: "Who sees this differently?" and "What is missing?" Silence is not agreement.

## Step 5 — Decision rules

Choose per decision type, before the session:

| Rule | Meaning | Use for | Avoid for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Decider after input | One named person decides after structured input (Rogers & Blenko's "D") | Prioritization, funding, scope trade-offs | Decisions the decider cannot enforce |
| Consent | Proceed unless someone raises a reasoned objection: an argument that the proposal causes harm or has a better alternative (Sociocracy 3.0) | Research plans, working agreements, next steps that are cheap to revisit | Irreversible commitments |
| Consensus | Everyone actively agrees (Kaner) | Small groups defining shared scope where buy-in is needed | Large groups; time-boxed sessions |
| Vote | Majority of those present | Choosing among options already judged acceptable | Anything that is a question of fact |

Facts are never decided by any rule. "Do technicians feel ready after training?" goes to research; "which stage do we research first?" can be decided.

## Step 6 — Remote, in-person, and hybrid

| Aspect | In-person | Remote |
|---|---|---|
| Session length | Up to half a day with breaks | Blocks of 90 minutes or less; split longer work across days |
| Silent generation | Sticky notes, color per tag | Board sections locked per person, or chat sent simultaneously |
| Evidence tags | Colored stickers | Pre-built note templates with a tag field |
| Pairs and fours | Move chairs | Breakout rooms with a named reporter |
| Record | Photograph every board section with note keys visible | Export the board; keep the version |
| Risk | Loud voices dominate | Silent participants disappear; camera fatigue |

Hybrid sessions disadvantage remote participants; if any participant is remote, run everyone on the remote tools.

## Step 7 — Participant power dynamics

- Senior participants speak last in each round and vote privately.
- Use anonymous input for topics where people report on their own managers or teams.
- For employee journeys, hold a separate session for frontline staff without their managers; psychological safety (Edmondson) determines whether errors and workarounds are reported at all. Bring themes, not names, to the joint session.
- Do not invite customers or employees as research participants into a stakeholder workshop unless the research design, consent, and data handling are in place; otherwise they become tokens.
- When one voice dominates, move to written rounds rather than asking the person to talk less.
- Record who holds each dissent position by role, not name, when the power gap is large.

## Step 8 — Convert outputs into registers afterwards

Within two working days:

1. **Transcribe** every board section with note keys (`W1-S2-07`: workshop 1, section 2, note 7). Keys go into `source_reference`.
2. **Evidence:** each `O` note is matched to its evidence ID; a participant record not yet registered becomes a new evidence row with its real source type. Each `H` note that matters to a decision becomes an evidence row with `source_type` `stakeholder-input` and status `hypothesis`. Each `U` becomes an evidence row with status `unknown` stating the question.
3. **Stages:** stage proposals become draft node rows with `evidence_status` no stronger than their tags (usually `hypothesis`).
4. **Moments:** candidate moments go to the moment candidate log, not the moment register.
5. **Opportunities:** problems become draft opportunity rows; without observed problems and causes their decision is `investigate`.
6. **Decisions:** the decision log records each decision, the rule used, the decider, and the dissent attached.
7. **Change log:** if a governed journey changed, write the change-log row with approver.
8. **Send back** the transcription to participants with a 3-day window for corrections of what they said, not what was decided.

## Failure-mode table

| Failure | Symptom | Prevention |
|---|---|---|
| Consensus mistaken for evidence | Map labeled "validated" after the workshop | Tags on every note; conversion rule in Step 8 |
| Anchoring | Everyone's estimate within 10% of the first spoken number | Private estimates before discussion |
| HiPPO | Final map matches the most senior participant's pre-read comments | Senior speaks last; private voting |
| Production blocking | Few ideas from a large group | Silent generation, brainwriting |
| Lost dissent | "Everyone agreed" in the notes; objections resurface later | Dissent log with owner and resolution path |
| Rule switching | Vote called when the decider dislikes the outcome | Decision rule fixed in the invitation |
| Workshop as endpoint | Board photos in a folder; no register rows | Step 8 within two days, owner named at close |
| Channel or team stages | Stages named after departments | Actor-goal prompts; stage-name check before convergence |

## Worked 120-minute session

Journey `JRN-EMP-TELCO-FIELD-START-001`, "Become a productive field technician". Decision: the Head of Field Operations decides which two stages get research and redesign next quarter (decider after input). Evidence pack: training completion records (`EVD-2026-0761`, observed), first-90-day job records (`EVD-2026-0762`, observed: median 11 weeks to first unsupervised job), six technician interviews (`EVD-2026-0763`). Remote session; 12 participants: 4 technicians hired this year (joined after a separate frontline session the week before), 3 team leaders, training, scheduling, HR, IT, and the Head of Field Operations. Pre-session estimates of "weeks to work alone" ranged from 4 to 12, median 6.

| Minutes | Activity | Technique | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–10 | Decision, rule, tags; evidence pack recap | — | Board shows decision statement and tag legend |
| 10–20 | Reveal pre-session estimates next to EVD-2026-0762 (median 11 weeks) | Independent estimates | Gap between belief (6) and record (11) noted; `U`: where do the extra weeks go? |
| 20–40 | Stages of the technician's first 90 days, each person alone, then pairs | Hidden first pass, 1-2-4 | 5 draft stages; stage 03 "Shadow a senior technician" appears in every version |
| 40–60 | Friction per stage: silent notes with tags, round-robin read-out | Nominal group | 41 notes: 7 `O`, 3 `I`, 26 `H`, 5 `U` |
| 60–70 | Break; evidence scribe checks `O` tags | — | 2 `O` retagged `H` (no source) |
| 70–85 | Disagreements | Dissent log | Team leaders: shadowing ends when the technician is ready; new technicians: it ends when a van becomes free. No `O` either side → research question |

## Quality checks

- Decision statement, decision rule, and decider fixed before the session.
- Pre-session independent input collected for problems and estimates.
- Every note carries a tag; `O` tags checked against the evidence pack.
- Dissent log kept, with owner and resolution path for each item.
- Power dynamics planned from the participant map.
- Register rows written within two working days; no workshop output raised above `hypothesis` without evidence.
